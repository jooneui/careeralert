## AI in Research
- [AI-Assisted Paper on Z-Library Cites a TorrentFreak Article That Doesn't Exist](https://torrentfreak.com/ai-assisted-paper-on-z-library-cites-a-torrentfreak-article-that-doesnt-exist/) — Source: primary reporting (TorrentFreak, published 2026-10-24). Two Nigerian-university librarians published a paper in the New Review of Academic Librarianship (Taylor & Francis) whose acknowledgements credit Consensus AI for literature sifting and ChatGPT for language help; TorrentFreak found at least 10 of 44 references could not be located (including a cited TorrentFreak article and a DOJ press release that do not exist) and an AI-text detector flagged the abstract and conclusion as likely fully AI-generated — the detector result is not proof AI wrote the fabricated citations, and Taylor & Francis has referred the matter to its integrity team with no retraction decision yet.

## Leaders on AI's Future
- [Jack Clark: "The singularity is here"](https://jack-clark.net/2026/10/19/import-ai-477-in-which-i-tell-the-imf-the-singularity-has-begun/) — Source: own newsletter (Import AI #477, published 2026-10-19, recapping his 2026-10-17 Per Jacobsson Foundation lecture to the IMF/World Bank meetings in Bangkok). Opinion: the Anthropic co-founder says "I believe that this year we have gained convincing evidence that it has now begun," citing AI progress on open math problems (Navier-Stokes, Jacobian Conjecture, several Erdős problems), AI-designed drug candidates, and ~10x internal code output at Anthropic; he models a scenario where heavy AI-driven knowledge work pushes US GDP to roughly $44 trillion by 2030, calling that figure conditional on fast diffusion and broad autonomous task completion. Some outlets note he paired this with saying humans still control AI today — a tension his critics flag given Anthropic's own commercial stake in the narrative.
