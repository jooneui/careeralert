## Performance & Benchmarks
- [A 10-year-old CNN outperforms frontier LLMs for skin cancer diagnosis](https://lynchlab.net/llm-skin-cancer) — Source: independent test by a practicing dermatologist (Dr. Magnus Lynch, published 2026-10-25); on 200 biopsy-confirmed lesions from the MILK10k dataset, a fine-tuned 2015-era Inception v3 CNN picked the correct first-choice diagnosis 62% of the time versus 42% for both GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1, though the LLMs beat the CNN on some specific diagnoses (e.g., basal cell carcinoma); this is one physician's informal n=200 test, not a maintained benchmark, and the author flags possible overlap between the public dataset and the LLMs' training data.

## Leaders on AI's Future
- [Yann LeCun: Dario Amodei is "completely deluded" about AI risk](https://thenextweb.com/news/yann-lecun-dario-amodei-deluded-rogue-agents-fortune) — Source: Fortune interview reported by The Next Web; Yann LeCun, AMI Labs founder and former Meta chief AI scientist, 2026-10-01/02. Opinion: LeCun said of Anthropic's CEO "I think he's completely deluded," has "zero concerns" about AI causing human extinction, and called "AI can kill us all" messaging "the worst marketing campaign you can possibly imagine," attributing recent rogue-agent incidents to poor sandboxing rather than inherent model danger — a direct counterpoint to Jack Clark's "singularity has begun" framing covered in the 2026-10-24 digest.

## Community Views
- [AI use leads UC Berkeley math professor to see "no point" in grading homework](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50233304) — Source: Hacker News (226 points, 320 comments, discussing a 2026-10-23 Daily Californian report); community opinion: many agreed homework grading has become a pointless transaction once AI makes it easy to fake mastery and floated ungraded/exam-only alternatives, while critics disputed the article's claim that professors pay TAs from grant funds and argued working problems by hand is still essential to learning math, with no consensus on whether grading itself (versus who grades) is the real issue.
- [The State of Context Engineering](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50235780) — Source: Hacker News (224 points, 57 comments, discussing dbreunig's 2026-10-24 essay); community opinion: praise focused on the framing that context organization matters as much as context length, with commenters sharing their own techniques (summary trees, append-only memory, deliberate forgetting/checkpointing); criticism challenged the specific degradation thresholds the essay cited, objected that "context poisoning" conflates model-weight knowledge with in-context data, and called some of the field's new terminology overwrought for what is still prompt engineering.
- [A 10-year-old CNN outperforms frontier LLMs for skin cancer diagnosis](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50233779) — Source: Hacker News (625 points, 260 comments, 2026-10-25); community opinion: many agreed narrow trained classifiers still beat general LLMs at scale and described workflows using LLMs only to label data for a cheaper downstream classifier, while others defended LLM "decision models" for zero-shot, low-stakes tasks and disputed whether the comparison was fair ("benchmaxxing" a narrow CNN) or clinically meaningful given overdiagnosis tradeoffs in dermatology.
