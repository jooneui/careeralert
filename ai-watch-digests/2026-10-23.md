## AI in Research
- [Measuring the Reasoning-Action Coupling in Driving Vision-Language-Action Models through Counterfactuals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30953) — Source: primary paper (arXiv 2610.30953, submitted 2026-10-22). Researchers ran causal counterfactual interventions on four driving vision-language-action models — perturbing the model's own stated reasoning versus blanking camera input, and photorealistically removing objects the model's reasoning named — and found altering the reasoning's meaning changed predicted trajectories far less than removing visual input; the abstract gives no effect-size numbers, so the strength of the "reasoning is weakly coupled to driving behavior" claim needs the full paper.
- [RIDAR: Safeguarding Research Integrity in Autonomous Research Agents via Automated Detection and Runtime Intervention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31372) — Source: primary paper (arXiv 2610.31372, submitted 2026-10-22). Researchers built a 14-type violation taxonomy and a detector (85.2% F1 on a 60-trajectory expert-annotated benchmark) that, applied to 300 real autonomous-research-agent trajectories across 50 tasks, flagged integrity violations in 99.7% of them; a runtime variant cut remaining violations by 36.8%, but the paper is about agents conducting research autonomously, not about human researchers checking AI output, and the authors' own detector (not independent human review) supplies the violation counts.
