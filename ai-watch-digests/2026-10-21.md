## Local LLMs
- [Muse Spark Open](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-spark-open) — Source: official (Meta); published 2026-10-15. 816B-total/42B-active natively multimodal MoE under Meta's Muse Model License (limited commercial terms); Meta states it ran on two Apple M5 Ultra Mac Studios (512GB unified memory each) via MLX, calling it "the most capable US open-weight model to date" — a vendor claim, not an independent score.
- [Beam](https://reflection.ai/blog/beam-is-available) — Source: official (Reflection AI); published 2026-10-16. Apache 2.0-licensed 501B-total/23B-active MoE built for coding, reasoning, and long-running agentic tasks, shipped in BF16/FP8/NVFP4 weights on Hugging Face; the post gives no formal benchmark table, only anecdotal demos, so treat "frontier reasoning efficiency" as unverified.
- [Timestral](https://mistral.ai/news/timestral/) — Source: official (Mistral AI); published 2026-10-15. Modified-MIT-licensed family of tiny (5M–209M parameter) time-series forecasting models for zero-shot multivariate forecasting, runnable locally via GitHub/Hugging Face; a specialized forecasting model rather than a general LLM, but a practical option for traffic/demand forecasting work given its small footprint.

## AI in Research
- [Large Language Model Assisted Transportation Planning through a Differentiable User Equilibrium Layer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16140) — Source: primary paper (arXiv 2610.16140, submitted 2026-10-12). Researchers pair an LLM (via MCP) with the open-source AequilibraE planning engine and a differentiable traffic-assignment layer (built by differentiating the Beckmann problem's KKT conditions) to let the LLM search/optimize travel-time calibration and network-design tasks rather than just query a simulator; the abstract reports no quantitative results or baselines, only two qualitative demonstrations.
- [ReviewPilot: A Multi-Agent System for Screening and Data Extraction in Systematic Reviews](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21042) — Source: primary paper (arXiv 2610.21042, submitted 2026-10-15). Researchers built a conversational multi-agent system automating PRISMA-style screening/extraction with a logged reason per screening decision and a source per extracted value; it beat a single-prompt LLM baseline and AgentSLR on epidemiology reviews (screening macro-F1 up to 0.776), but the quantitative validation is limited to epidemiology, with HCI/biomedicine covered only as qualitative case studies.

## Community Views
- [Hybrid Reasoning Agents paper discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50145337) — Source: Hacker News; community opinion (2026-10-18): commenters praised the open release of weights/code and the intuition that reasoning needn't be token-granular, while the dominant criticism was that latent-space reasoning weakens auditability/monitorability of agent thinking, with some early testers reporting the model looping on simple prompts. Individual opinions, not benchmark evidence.
- [Show HN: Qwen3-4B with the AI writing tics trained out](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50151928) — Source: Hacker News; community opinion (2026-10-19): commenters liked that a 4B model was de-"sloped" via LoRA distillation on consumer hardware (an M2 MacBook Air), but several said the output still reads as AI-written or got longer/more bloated, and some disputed whether em dashes or "lists of three" are even stylistic flaws worth removing.
- [Websites are closing their doors to AI agents](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50141657) — Source: Hacker News; community opinion (2026-10-18): commenters largely defended site operators' right to block agent traffic over bandwidth/revenue costs, while the main criticism was that bot-protection tools (e.g., Cloudflare-style WAFs) cause collateral damage to legitimate human users and further centralize the web around a few vendors.
