## Research Evidence
- [How Do Waymo Self-Driving Cars Impact Traffic Flow? Evidence from Empirical Data](https://doi.org/10.1007/s42421-026-00177-1) — Source: Data Science for Transportation (peer-reviewed); published 2026-10-20; Danjue Chen, Tienan Li, Jorge Laval, Soyoung Ahn, Zuduo Zheng. Data: large-scale Waymo self-driving-car driving data in mixed traffic. Method: identify stationary traffic states, estimate spacing–speed and flow–density relations for Waymo cars vs human drivers. Finding: Waymo cars keep larger gaps, cutting capacity ~18% on 40-mph arterials; human drivers near them also become more conservative; larger jam spacing may reduce storage capacity. Limitation: arterial settings with a single AV operator.
- [User Perceptions of Robotaxis and Mode Choice Among Robotaxi, Transit, and Ridesharing](https://doi.org/10.1177/03611981261487420) — Source: Transportation Research Record (peer-reviewed); published 2026-10-13; Xiaoxia Dong et al. (with Zhongjie Lin, Erick Guerra, Ziyi Tang, Yadan Luo). Data: survey of 411 robotaxi riders in Phoenix and San Francisco. Method: descriptive statistics plus mixed logit mode-choice models. Finding: robotaxis appear to be substituting for ridesharing and transit trips, with in-vehicle time rated less burdensome than transit or rideshare and choice likelihood varying by gender, income, and education. Limitation: self-selected survey of existing robotaxi users, so results may not generalize to non-users.
- [How Lower Fares and Price Certainty Shape Shared Micromobility Use: The Effect of Flat Rates on Trip Frequency, Distance, and Speed](https://doi.org/10.32866/001c.172176) — Source: Findings, Transport Findings section (peer-reviewed); published 2026-10-20; Tatsuya Fukushige et al. (with Hope Bodenschatz, Calvin Thigpen, Kari Watkins, Dillon T. Fitch-Polse). Data: randomized Lime flat-rate pricing trial among infrequent/non-users in San Francisco, Oakland, Sacramento, and Los Angeles (summer and winter 2025). Method: Bayesian multilevel difference-in-differences models on trip transaction records. Finding: flat-rate adopters took significantly longer trips than control riders, while trip frequency and speed showed no clear effect in the intent-to-treat analysis. Limitation: low flat-rate uptake and a short trial window limited power to detect frequency effects.
- [Estimating Pedestrian Flows and Signal Delay from Sydney Traffic-Signal Event Histories](https://doi.org/10.32866/001c.172160) — Source: Findings, Transport Findings section (peer-reviewed); published 2026-10-20; David M. Levinson. Data: Sydney SCATS traffic-signal controller event histories across 229 intersections, screened to 5,114 movement-hours. Method: first-actuation estimator converting pedestrian call/button-press timing into arrival-rate and delay estimates, calibrated against a companion study. Finding: ~331,729 estimated pedestrian crossings with ~3,671 pedestrian-hours of delay (39.8 s/crossing) under full compliance, falling to ~29.8 s/crossing under a behavior-adjusted scenario. Limitation: calibration transfer to these sites is unverified and individual compliance/travel direction are unobserved.
